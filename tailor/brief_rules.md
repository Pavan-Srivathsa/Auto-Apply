# Job brief rules

Read the posting once, carefully, and decide what this employer is really
hiring for. Then say what the candidate has that answers each thing.

## Requirements

- At most eight, ranked: the thing the posting leans on hardest first. Use the
  title and level, the "required" and "minimum" lists, the first responsibilities
  and anything repeated. Skip company boilerplate, benefits, legal text and
  anything about visas, salary or location.
- `must` is something the posting requires or builds the role around. `nice`
  is preferred, a plus, or mentioned once in passing.
- `text` is short and in the posting's words: "Build and operate data
  pipelines in Python", not a paraphrase.
- `terms` are the exact skills, tools or phrases the posting uses for it, in
  its spelling ("PostgreSQL", not "databases"). They are what a recruiter
  scanning the resume looks for.

## Evidence

- `evidence` is one line copied **exactly** from the resume or the profile
  that best answers the requirement. Copy it; do not tidy, merge or shorten
  it. A quote that is not found in the sources is thrown away and the
  requirement becomes a gap.
- If nothing there answers it, leave `evidence` empty. A gap is a useful,
  honest answer; a stretch is not. Never count an adjacent skill as the
  skill, and never take evidence from the posting.
